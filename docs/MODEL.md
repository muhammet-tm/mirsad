# Model: DaE2, reproduced, tested, and extended

Mirsad's detection engine implements DaE2, the diverse and efficient ensemble of Omolara and Alawida (Computers & Security 148, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.cose.2024.104170). Dr. Moatsum Alawida (Abu Dhabi University), the paper's corresponding author, mentored the team. This document reports what our own code measured. The paper reports 98.5% accuracy for AdaBoost on its test split; that figure belongs to the paper, and every other number below comes from `results/*.json`, generated by `scripts/train_eval.py` and filled in by `scripts/build_docs.py`.

## What DaE2 is

The paper turns each URL into TF-IDF features, reduces them with PCA, trains several classical learners (SVM, random forest, logistic regression, decision tree, naive Bayes, KNN), and compares four ensembles: AdaBoost, Bagging, Stacking with a logistic-regression meta-learner, and soft Voting over KNN, logistic regression, and SVM. It then keeps the ensemble with the best balance of accuracy and speed.

Our implementation (`mirsad/models/dae2.py`) keeps that design and fixes the choices the paper leaves open: word TF-IDF of the raw URL for the faithful reproduction ("paper" representation); character 3- to 5-grams of the canonical URL for our version ("ours"), with the scheme and "www." removed; chi-squared selection of 20,000 columns before a 100-component truncated SVD (the sparse-matrix form of PCA); 25 handcrafted lexical features beside the SVD components; AdaBoost over logistic regression (the best AdaBoost row of the paper's Table 4); and a KNN fitted on a 50,000-row sample so live scoring stays fast. The deployed score is the mean of the four ensembles' probabilities, and the threshold is fixed on validation data at a false-positive rate of at most 1%.

## Protocol

Written before any run, and applied to every experiment:

- Thresholds are chosen on a 10% validation slice of the training data and frozen before the test set is scored.
- Results report precision, recall, F1, and the false-positive rate for the fraud class, plus ROC AUC and the precision that the same recall and false-positive rate would give if 1% of screened links were fraudulent. Macro and weighted averages are stored separately in the result files and never mixed in one table.
- Each experiment trains on a stratified sample of up to 200,000 rows because of the 24-hour build; the result files record the exact row counts.

## E0: what the dataset rewards

Before training, `scripts/audit_dataset.py` measured malicious_phish (651,191 rows):

- A single rule, "malicious if the URL starts with a scheme", is 83.8% accurate, because 68.4% of malicious rows carry a scheme against 8.3% of benign rows. Every defacement URL does.
- Under the paper's random 80/20 split, 80.9% of test rows have their registered domain in the training set.
- The defacement class has 96,457 rows from only 2,122 registered domains.
- There are 10,072 exact duplicate URLs and 4,017 canonical URLs that carry two different labels.

These are the reasons for the canonical representation and the domain split below.

## E4: speed

On the development laptop (AMD Ryzen 5 4600H, one process), the deployed model scores one URL in a median of 114.73 ms (95th percentile 127.45 ms) and about 2,060 URLs a second in batches, which is far above what the collectors produce.

## The second stage

A URL string cannot show a domain's age, a card form, or a logo. Those signals come from passive enrichment and the sandbox, and they enter as rules in `mirsad/scoring/engine.py` that can only raise the risk (except the official-domain cap). The rules are hand-set floors, not learned weights; learning them needs labeled live cases, which the analyst decisions in the audit log will provide over time.

The E3 table shows why a model score alone is not enough on live traffic: if 1% of screened links were fraudulent, only about 41.9% of the model's flags would be right. The first live runs confirmed it: legitimate UAE business sites from map listings scored above the threshold on the URL alone. So on the live channels (search, maps, certificates) a link enters the analyst queue on the model alone only when the mean score is at least 0.97; below that, at least one piece of evidence (a brand on a non-official host, a card or password form, a logo on the wrong domain, a reputation listing, a reused phone number, a paid ad for a brand query) must agree. Links that miss the bar stay just under the threshold, on watch. Links an analyst checks by hand, and feed items, are scored without this bar. Hosts under .gov.ae are treated as government-owned, because the .ae registry restricts those names to government bodies.

## Limits

- Training used samples of up to 200,000 rows, not the full datasets, because of the 24-hour build.
- malicious_phish reflects URLs from roughly 2007 to 2021 from mixed sources; PhreshPhish ends in December 2025.
- At realistic prevalence, precision falls sharply even for good models; the queue ranks cases and an officer confirms each one.
- The brand list has 31 brands; only 3 official domains are verified from a source.
- This document reports only our own measurements under the protocol above; it does not re-analyze the paper's tables.
