Mirsad

Finding fraudulent links in search results and maps before victims do

TeamMuhammet Yalkapov (lead), Sulaymon Sadullo, and Ahmed Mohamed Almheiri, Abu Dhabi University

Mirsad means the watchtower. It watches the places where people in the UAE meet fraudulent links, and it hands the police evidence before victims report. The detection engine is DaE2, published by Omolara and Alawida in 2025; Dr. Alawida mentors our team, and all code is our own.

The UAE already protects people at the moment of contact. RZAM blocks known sites in the browser, Digital FraudHunter checks a message, and Aman takes the report. Mirsad works one step earlier, where the scammer places the link, and it complements these tools rather than replacing them.

How Mirsad works

01Discover
  • Search results and ads (23 UAE service queries, English and Arabic)
  • Map listings (Google Places, Abu Dhabi)
  • New certificates (lookalikes of 31 UAE brands)
  • Threat feeds (OpenPhish, URLhaus, PhishStats)
02Enrich(passive only)
  • Domain age (RDAP, first certificate)
  • Reputation (URLhaus, VirusTotal)
  • Sandbox capture in an isolated VM
  • Phishpedia logo match
03Score
  • Stage 1, DaE2 ensemble on every URL (AdaBoost, Bagging, Stacking, Voting)
  • Stage 2, evidence rules that only raise risk
  • Official-domain cap
  • Plain-language reasons
04Act
  • Risk-ranked analyst queue
  • Case file with numbered exhibits
  • Hold to confirm
  • Evidence package for a registrar, aeDA, or a police blocking request

The host laptop never loads a suspicious page. Summaries by Jais (or a local Falcon) never decide.

Four stages. Discover collects links from the four channels. Enrich adds passive evidence only: domain age, reputation, a sandbox capture in an isolated VM, and a logo match. Score runs DaE2 on every URL and then evidence rules that can only raise the risk. Act gives the analyst a queue, a case file, and an evidence package.
Model choices
07 / 14

DaE2, reproduced first, then hardened

What it is

DaE2 combines four ensembles (AdaBoost, Bagging, Stacking, and Voting) over character patterns and 25 lexical features of the URL. Omolara and Alawida published it in 2025.

Why an ensemble

Fast enough to screen every link on a laptop CPU, explainable through four votes per link, and steadier than any one of its models alone.

What we changed

We reproduced the paper's setting first, then changed only what our data audit showed was wrong: canonical URLs, a split by domain, retraining on 2024 to 2025 data, and a threshold fixed at a 1% false-positive rate.

This is the slide that earns trust. The paper's dataset rewards recognizing which list a URL came from: one rule about the scheme already scores 83.8%. On never-seen sites accuracy drops to 84.1%. On real later traffic, a model trained the paper's way flags most legitimate sites, with a false-positive rate of 88.6%. Our deployed model keeps false alarms near 1% on months it never saw.

Beyond the URL: evidence an officer can read

  1. 01Domain age, from RDAP and the first certificate
  2. 02A card or password form on the page
  3. 03A form that posts to another domain
  4. 04A brand's logo on a domain the brand does not own
  5. 05A paid ad or top result for a brand query, off the brand's domain
  6. 06A phone number shared across map listings

Each rule can only raise the risk, and each adds a plain reason, such as "Domain first seen 4 days ago".

41.9%

If 1% of links are fraudulent, even this model is right on about this share of its flags. The queue therefore ranks cases, and an officer confirms each one.

A URL alone is not enough. At a realistic prevalence of 1% fraudulent links, even a strong model would be right on about 41.9% of its flags. So on live channels a link needs either a very high model score or one piece of evidence that agrees, and every case reaches an officer with plain reasons.
Privacy design
10 / 14

The subject is websites, not people

Suspicious pages load only in an isolated VM. The host receives a screenshot and the page facts, never the page itself.

  • No visitor data, cookies, or form inputs are collected
  • Query strings that may carry victim data are stored only as keyed hashes
  • Inside the sandbox, private addresses are blocked and no form is ever submitted
  • Summaries come from Mirsad's own facts, never the attacker's page text; a local Falcon gives a zero-egress mode
  • Every view, decision, and export is written to an append-only audit log
  • An officer decides; nothing is blocked automatically

Legal frame: Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021. Articles 11, 15, 40, and 48 describe what Mirsad detects; Articles 1 to 4 and 12 bound how it collects; Articles 62 and 66 are the powers its evidence package serves. Principles of the Personal Data Protection Law are applied by design.

Privacy by design: the subject is websites, not people. Victim data that can hide in query strings is stored only as keyed hashes. Suspicious pages load only in an isolated VM. The language model never reads the attacker's page, so a page cannot instruct it. Every action is audited, and an officer decides.
